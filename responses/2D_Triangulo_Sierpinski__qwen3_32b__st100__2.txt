To determine the coordinates $(x, y)$ corresponding to a given integer $n$, based on the described pattern, we follow a structured process:

---

### **Step 1: Compute the Cumulative Count Function $S(y)$**

We define $S(y)$ as the cumulative number of points up to $y$. Each integer $i$ from $0$ to $y$ contributes $2^k$ points, where $k$ is the number of 1s in the binary representation of $i$. That is:

$$
S(y) = \sum_{i=0}^{y} 2^{\text{number of 1s in binary(i)}}
$$

This sum helps us identify the range of $n$ values corresponding to a specific $y$.

---

### **Step 2: Identify the Correct $y$ for the Given $n$**

We find the smallest $y$ such that:

$$
S(y - 1) \leq n < S(y)
$$

This $y$ will determine the binary structure from which we generate the $x$-coordinate.

---

### **Step 3: Extract the Binary Positions of 1s in $y$**

From the binary representation of $y$, identify the positions of the 1s (0-indexed from right to left). These positions will be used to generate all possible subsets.

---

### **Step 4: Generate All Subsets of the Binary Positions**

Each subset corresponds to a binary number formed by setting the bits at those positions to 1. The decimal value of each subset is calculated by summing $2^{\text{position}}$ for each position in the subset.

---

### **Step 5: Sort the Subsets and Select the $x$-Coordinate**

Sort the generated numbers in ascending order. The $x$-coordinate is the number at the index:

$$
n - S(y - 1)
$$

---

### **Example: $n = 10$**

1. **Compute $S(y)$:**
   - $S(0) = 1$
   - $S(1) = 3$
   - $S(2) = 5$
   - $S(3) = 9$
   - $S(4) = 11$

2. **Find $y$:**
   - $S(3) = 9 \leq 10 < S(4) = 11$, so $y = 4$

3. **Binary of $y = 4$ is $100$** → positions of 1s are $[2]$

4. **Generate subsets of $[2]$:**
   - $[2] \rightarrow 4$
   - $[] \rightarrow 0$

   Subsets: $[0, 4]$

5. **Offset = $10 - 9 = 1$** → $x = 4$

---

### **Final Answer for $n = 10$:**

$$
\boxed{(4, 4)}
$$
