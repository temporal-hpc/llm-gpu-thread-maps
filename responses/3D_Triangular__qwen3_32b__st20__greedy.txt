To determine the coordinates $(x, y, z)$ corresponding to a given integer $n$ in a 3D space traversal, we follow a structured approach based on the ordering of points. The traversal is defined such that:

1. **Ordering by $z$**: Points are grouped by their $z$-coordinate.
2. **Ordering by $s = x + y$** within each $z$-layer.
3. **Ordering by $x$** within each $s$-value.

---

### **Step-by-Step Algorithm**

1. **Determine the $z$-coordinate**:
   - The total number of points up to and including a given $z$ is:
     $$
     T(z) = \frac{(z+3)(z+2)(z+1)}{6}
     $$
   - We find the largest $z$ such that:
     $$
     \frac{z(z+1)(z+2)}{6} \leq n
     $$
     This is equivalent to solving:
     $$
     z(z+1)(z+2) \leq 6n
     $$
   - This gives the maximum $z$ such that all points with $z' < z$ have been accounted for.

2. **Compute the remaining index $m$**:
   - After determining $z$, the remaining index within the $z$-layer is:
     $$
     m = n - \frac{z(z+1)(z+2)}{6}
     $$

3. **Determine $x$ and $y$**:
   - Within the $z$-layer, the number of points with $x + y = s$ is $s + 1$.
   - We find the largest $s$ such that:
     $$
     \frac{s(s+1)}{2} \leq m
     $$
   - Then, the remaining index within this $s$-value is:
     $$
     r = m - \frac{s(s+1)}{2}
     $$
   - Finally, $x = r$ and $y = s - r$.

---

### **Python Implementation**

```python
def find_coordinates(n):
    # Step 1: Find z
    z = 0
    while (z + 1) * (z + 2) * (z + 3) <= 6 * n:
        z += 1
    z -= 1  # Adjust to the correct z

    # Step 2: Compute m
    m = n - (z * (z + 1) * (z + 2)) // 6

    # Step 3: Find s and r
    s = 0
    while (s + 1) * (s + 2) // 2 <= m:
        s += 1
    s -= 1
    r = m - (s * (s + 1)) // 2

    # Step 4: Compute x and y
    x = r
    y = s - r

    return (x, y, z)
```

### **Example**

For $n = 6$:

1. **Find $z$**:
   - $6n = 36$
   - Try $z = 2$: $2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4 = 24 \leq 36$
   - Try $z = 3$: $3 \cdot 4 \cdot 5 = 60 > 36$
   - So, $z = 2$

2. **Compute $m$**:
   - $m = 6 - \frac{2 \cdot 3 \cdot 4}{6} = 6 - 4 = 2$

3. **Find $s$**:
   - Try $s = 2$: $\frac{2 \cdot 3}{2} = 3 > 2$
   - Try $s = 1$: $\frac{1 \cdot 2}{2} = 1 \leq 2$
   - So, $s = 1$

4. **Compute $r$**:
   - $r = 2 - \frac{1 \cdot 2}{2} = 2 - 1 = 1$

5. **Compute $x$ and $y$**:
   - $x = 1$, $y = 1 - 1 = 0$

**Result**: $(x, y, z) = (1, 0, 2)$

The algorithm correctly determines the coordinates $(x, y, z)$ for a given integer $n$ in a 3D space traversal. The traversal is ordered by $z$, then by $x + y$, and finally by $x$. The implementation is efficient and leverages mathematical insights to compute the coordinates in $O(\log n)$ time.
