To determine the coordinates $(x, y)$ corresponding to a given integer $n$, we observe that the numbers are arranged in a triangular pattern. Each diagonal (or level) corresponds to a fixed $y$ value, and the numbers within that diagonal range from $x = 0$ to $x = y$.

---

### Step-by-step Derivation

1. **Pattern Observation**:
   - The numbers are arranged in diagonals where each diagonal corresponds to a fixed $y$.
   - For a given $y$, the values of $x$ range from $0$ to $y$, and the total number of elements in the first $y$ diagonals is:
     $$
     T(y) = \frac{y(y + 1)}{2}
     $$
     This is the sum of the first $y$ natural numbers.

2. **Finding the Correct Diagonal (y)**:
   - We want to find the largest $y$ such that:
     $$
     T(y) = \frac{y(y + 1)}{2} \leq n
     $$
   - Solving for $y$ in this inequality gives:
     $$
     y = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor
     $$

3. **Finding the x-coordinate**:
   - Once $y$ is determined, the starting index of that diagonal is $T(y)$.
   - The offset within that diagonal is:
     $$
     x = n - \frac{y(y + 1)}{2}
     $$

---

### Final Formula

Given an integer $n$, the coordinates $(x, y)$ are computed as follows:

- $$
  y = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor
  $$
- $$
  x = n - \frac{y(y + 1)}{2}
  $$
