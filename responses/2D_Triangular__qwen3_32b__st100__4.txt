To determine the coordinates $(x, y)$ corresponding to a given integer $n$, we observe a pattern in the sequence of coordinates:

$$
\begin{array}{c|c|c|c}
n & x & y & \text{Sum } x + y \\
\hline
0 & 0 & 0 & 0 \\
1 & 0 & 1 & 1 \\
2 & 1 & 1 & 2 \\
3 & 0 & 2 & 2 \\
4 & 1 & 2 & 3 \\
5 & 2 & 2 & 4 \\
6 & 0 & 3 & 3 \\
7 & 1 & 3 & 4 \\
8 & 2 & 3 & 5 \\
9 & 3 & 3 & 6 \\
10 & 0 & 4 & 4 \\
\end{array}
$$

From this, we notice that the coordinates form layers where each layer $k$ (starting from 0) contains exactly $k+1$ elements. The starting index of each layer is the $k$-th triangular number:

$$
T(k) = \frac{k(k+1)}{2}
$$

Given an integer $n$, the process to find $(x, y)$ is as follows:

---

### **Step 1: Determine the Layer $k$**

The layer $k$ is the largest integer such that the triangular number $T(k) = \frac{k(k+1)}{2} \leq n$.

This can be computed as:

$$
k = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor
$$

---

### **Step 2: Compute the Offset Within the Layer**

Once $k$ is known, the offset $m$ within the layer is:

$$
m = n - T(k) = n - \frac{k(k+1)}{2}
$$

---

### **Step 3: Determine the Coordinates**

In layer $k$, the $m$-th element has:

$$
x = m, \quad y = k
$$

---

### **Final Formula**

For a given $n \geq 0$, the coordinates $(x, y)$ are:

$$
k = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor, \quad
x = n - \frac{k(k+1)}{2}, \quad
y = k
$$

---

### **Example Calculation**

Let $n = 7$:

1. Compute $k = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8 \cdot 7 + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{57} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{7.55 - 1}{2} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor 3.27 \right\rfloor = 3$

2. Compute $T(k) = \frac{3 \cdot 4}{2} = 6$

3. Compute $m = 7 - 6 = 1$

4. Coordinates: $x = 1$, $y = 3$ → $(1, 3)$

---

### **Summary**

This method allows you to compute the coordinates $(x, y)$ for any non-negative integer $n$ by determining the appropriate triangular layer and offset within that layer. The result is a unique mapping from $n$ to a 2D grid point.
