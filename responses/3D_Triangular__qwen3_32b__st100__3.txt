To determine the position of the **n-th term** in a sequence that enumerates all elements of the upper triangular part of matrices in increasing size (i.e., 1×1, 2×2, 3×3, etc.), we follow a structured approach rooted in combinatorial mathematics.

---

### 📌 Problem Overview

We want to find the coordinates **(row, column)** of the **n-th element** in the sequence that lists all elements of the upper triangular part of matrices of increasing size, row-wise.

Each **m × m** matrix contributes $ \frac{m(m+1)}{2} $ elements to the sequence (the number of elements in the upper triangle, including the diagonal).

The **cumulative number of elements** up to the m × m matrix is:

$$
T(m) = \frac{m(m+1)(m+2)}{6}
$$

This is known as the **tetrahedral number**.

---

### ✅ Step-by-Step Solution

#### **Step 1: Determine Matrix Size m**

Find the smallest integer $ m $ such that:

$$
T(m-1) \leq n < T(m)
$$

That is,

$$
\frac{(m-1)m(m+1)}{6} \leq n < \frac{m(m+1)(m+2)}{6}
$$

This tells us that the n-th term lies within the **m × m matrix**.

#### **Step 2: Calculate Offset Within the m × m Matrix**

Let:

$$
\text{offset} = n - T(m-1)
$$

This gives the position of the term **within** the upper triangle of the m × m matrix.

#### **Step 3: Determine Row and Column**

Now, we need to find the row $ r $ and column $ c $ such that the element at position `offset` corresponds to the element at (r, c) in the upper triangle of the m × m matrix.

To do this, we iterate over rows 0 to m-1 and sum the number of elements per row until we exceed the offset.

- The number of elements in row $ r $ is $ m - r $
- So, the cumulative count up to row $ r $ is:

$$
\text{sum} = \sum_{i=0}^{r} (m - i) = \frac{(m + r + 1)(m - r)}{2}
$$

Use binary search or a simple loop to find the smallest $ r $ such that the cumulative sum is greater than the offset.

Let $ r $ be the row such that the sum up to row $ r-1 $ is less than or equal to the offset, and the sum up to row $ r $ is greater than the offset.

Then, the column is:

$$
c = \text{offset} - \sum_{i=0}^{r-1} (m - i)
$$

---

### 🧠 Example: Find the 10th Term

- $ T(3) = 10 $, $ T(2) = 4 $, so $ m = 3 $
- $ \text{offset} = 10 - 4 = 6 $
- Row 0: 3 elements → cumulative = 3
- Row 1: 2 elements → cumulative = 5
- Row 2: 1 element → cumulative = 6

So, row = 2, column = 0 → element is at (2, 2)

---

### ✅ Final Answer

To find the position of the **n-th term**, compute:

- Find $ m $ such that $ T(m-1) \leq n < T(m) $
- Compute $ \text{offset} = n - T(m-1) $
- Determine the row $ r $ and column $ c $ within the m × m matrix using the cumulative row sums

The final coordinates are:

$$
\boxed{(r, c)}
$$
