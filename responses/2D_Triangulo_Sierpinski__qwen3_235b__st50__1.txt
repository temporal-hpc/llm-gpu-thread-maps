The recurrence relation is given as \(b(2k) = 2b(k) + c(k)\) with \(b(1) = 1\). However, \(c(k)\) is not specified. In many standard problems, particularly when defining the identity function, \(c(k) = 0\) for the even case, and for odd indices, the recurrence \(b(2k+1) = 2b(k) + 1\) is commonly used.

Assuming \(c(k) = 0\) and the standard recurrence for odd indices:
- \(b(1) = 1\)
- For even \(n = 2k\), \(b(2k) = 2b(k)\)
- For odd \(n = 2k+1\), \(b(2k+1) = 2b(k) + 1\)

Computing the first few values:
- \(b(1) = 1\)
- \(b(2) = b(2 \cdot 1) = 2b(1) = 2 \cdot 1 = 2\)
- \(b(3) = b(2 \cdot 1 + 1) = 2b(1) + 1 = 2 \cdot 1 + 1 = 3\)
- \(b(4) = b(2 \cdot 2) = 2b(2) = 2 \cdot 2 = 4\)
- \(b(5) = b(2 \cdot 2 + 1) = 2b(2) + 1 = 2 \cdot 2 + 1 = 5\)

This pattern holds for all positive integers \(n\), yielding \(b(n) = n\).

Given that \(c(k)\) is not specified and the simplest and most common case (where \(c(k) = 0\) and the identity function is defined) results in \(b(n) = n\), this is the intended solution.
