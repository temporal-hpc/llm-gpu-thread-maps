To determine the coordinates $(x, y)$ of a point in a spiral traversal pattern for a given index $n$, we can use the following mathematical approach:

---

### **Step-by-Step Explanation**

1. **Understand the Spiral Pattern**:
   - The spiral is constructed such that each "layer" adds a new ring of points.
   - The positions are ordered in a way that can be modeled using **triangular numbers**. For a given layer $y$, the number of points in the ring is $8y$.

2. **Determine the Layer $y$**:
   - The layer $y$ corresponds to the largest integer such that the cumulative number of points up to layer $y$ is less than $n$.
   - Mathematically, solve for $y$ in the inequality:
     $$
     y(y + 1) < 2n \leq (y + 1)(y + 2)
     $$
   - This can be derived by solving the quadratic equation:
     $$
     y = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} - \epsilon \right\rfloor
     $$
     where $\epsilon$ is a small positive value (e.g., $10^{-9}$) to handle exact squares.

3. **Calculate the Offset $x$**:
   - Once $y$ is determined, compute the offset within the current layer:
     $$
     x = n - \frac{y(y + 1)}{2} - 1
     $$
   - This gives the position $x$ within the current ring.

---

### **Final Formulas**

Given an index $n$, the coordinates $(x, y)$ are computed as:

1. **Layer $y$**:
   $$
   y = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} - \epsilon \right\rfloor
   $$
   (where $\epsilon$ is a small positive number, e.g., $10^{-9}$)

2. **Offset $x$**:
   $$
   x = n - \frac{y(y + 1)}{2} - 1
   $$

---

### **Example**

Let’s compute $(x, y)$ for $n = 10$:

1. **Compute $y$**:
   $$
   \sqrt{8 \times 10 + 1} = \sqrt{81} = 9
   $$
   $$
   y = \left\lfloor \frac{9 - 1}{2} - 10^{-9} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor 4 - 10^{-9} \right\rfloor = 3
   $$

2. **Compute $x$**:
   $$
   x = 10 - \frac{3 \times 4}{2} - 1 = 10 - 6 - 1 = 3
   $$

**Result**: $(x, y) = (3, 3)$

---

### **Key Notes**

- The spiral pattern assumes that each layer $y$ has $8y$ points, and the cumulative number of points up to layer $y$ is $\frac{y(y + 1)}{2}$.
- The formulas work for any $n \geq 1$, and the coordinates $(x, y)$ correspond to the position in the spiral.
