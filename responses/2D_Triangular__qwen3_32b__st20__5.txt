To determine the coordinates $(x, y)$ for a given integer $n$ in a triangular numbering system (where elements are arranged in rows of increasing length: row 0 has 1 element, row 1 has 2 elements, row 2 has 3, and so on), we follow a mathematical derivation based on the properties of triangular numbers.

---

### **Step 1: Understanding the Pattern**

In a triangular layout, the number of elements up to and including row $y$ is given by the triangular number:

$$
T_y = \frac{y(y+1)}{2}
$$

This means that all elements from row 0 to row $y$ are contained in the first $T_y$ elements. So, to find the row $y$ in which the $n$-th element is located, we need to find the largest $y$ such that:

$$
\frac{y(y+1)}{2} \leq n
$$

---

### **Step 2: Solving for $y$**

We solve the inequality:

$$
\frac{y(y+1)}{2} \leq n
$$

Multiply both sides by 2:

$$
y^2 + y \leq 2n
$$

Solving the quadratic equation $y^2 + y - 2n = 0$ using the quadratic formula:

$$
y = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 8n}}{2}
$$

Since we want the largest integer $y$ such that the inequality holds, we take the **floor** of this expression:

$$
y = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor
$$

---

### **Step 3: Solving for $x$**

Once $y$ is determined, the index within the row is:

$$
x = n - \frac{y(y+1)}{2}
$$

---

### **Final Answer**

$$
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
y &= \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor \\
x &= n - \frac{y(y+1)}{2}
\end{aligned}
}
$$

This pair $(x, y)$ gives the coordinates of the $n$-th element in a triangular grid.
