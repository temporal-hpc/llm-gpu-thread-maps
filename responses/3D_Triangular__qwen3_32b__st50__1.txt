To determine the pair $(a, b)$ such that:

$$
N = a + \frac{b(b+1)}{2}
$$

we follow a method based on the properties of triangular numbers.

---

### Step-by-Step Explanation

1. **Understand the Structure**:
   The number $N$ is expressed as the sum of an integer $a$ and a triangular number $\frac{b(b+1)}{2}$. Triangular numbers are of the form:

   $$
   T_b = \frac{b(b+1)}{2}
   $$

   So, the task is to find the largest triangular number less than $N$, and then compute $a$ as the difference.

2. **Find an Approximate Value of $b$**:
   We want to find the largest integer $b$ such that:

   $$
   \frac{b(b+1)}{2} < N
   $$

   Solving the inequality:

   $$
   b(b+1) < 2N
   $$

   This is a quadratic equation in $b$, and solving it gives:

   $$
   b = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8N + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor
   $$

   This gives an initial estimate $b_0$.

3. **Adjust $b$ if Needed**:
   After computing $b_0$, we must verify whether $T_{b_0} = \frac{b_0(b_0+1)}{2}$ is less than $N$. If it is not, then we decrement $b_0$ by 1.

4. **Compute $a$**:
   Once we have the correct $b$, compute $a$ as:

   $$
   a = N - \frac{b(b+1)}{2}
   $$

---

### Final Algorithm

Given an integer $N$, the pair $(a, b)$ can be found as follows:

1. Compute:
   $$
   b_0 = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8N + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor
   $$

2. If $b_0(b_0 + 1)/2 \geq N$, then set $b = b_0 - 1$; otherwise, $b = b_0$.

3. Compute:
   $$
   a = N - \frac{b(b+1)}{2}
   $$

---

### Example

Let $N = 10$.

1. Compute:
   $$
   b_0 = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{81} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor = \left\lfloor \frac{9 - 1}{2} \right\rfloor = 4
   $$

2. Check $T_4 = 10 \geq 10$, so set $b = 3$.

3. Compute:
   $$
   a = 10 - \frac{3 \cdot 4}{2} = 10 - 6 = 4
   $$

So, the pair is $(a, b) = (4, 3)$, and indeed:

$$
4 + \frac{3 \cdot 4}{2} = 4 + 6 = 10
$$

---

### Summary

The solution involves computing the largest triangular number less than $N$ and then finding the difference to get the corresponding $a$. The process is efficient and ensures correctness for any positive integer $N$.
