To determine the **level** $ y $ such that a given integer $ n $ lies within the range of elements assigned to that level, we can model the problem using **triangular numbers**.

---

### 🧠 Problem Understanding

Assume that:
- Each **level $ y $** contains $ y + 1 $ elements.
- The total number of elements up to and including level $ y $ is the sum $ 1 + 2 + \dots + (y+1) $, which is the **triangular number** $ T_{y+1} = \frac{(y+1)(y+2)}{2} $.
- The cumulative number of elements **up to level $ y $** is $ S(y) = \frac{(y+1)(y+2)}{2} $.

Given a number $ n $, we want to find the **level $ y $** such that:

$$
S(y-1) < n \leq S(y)
$$

This is equivalent to finding the smallest $ y $ such that:

$$
\frac{(y+1)(y+2)}{2} > n
$$

---

### ✅ Solution Derivation

Let’s define $ k = y + 1 $. Then the condition becomes:

$$
\frac{k(k+1)}{2} > n
$$

To find the smallest such $ k $, we solve:

$$
k = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor + 1
$$

Then, the corresponding level is:

$$
y = k - 1
$$

Substituting back:

$$
y = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor
$$

---

### ✅ Final Answer

$$
\boxed{\left\lfloor \dfrac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor}
$$
