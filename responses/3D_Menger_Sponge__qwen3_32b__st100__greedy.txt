To solve the problem of finding the **maximum distance from a given point (x, y)** to **any point on the perimeter of a square**, we must first **clarify the assumptions** about the square. Since the problem does not explicitly define the square, we must make a reasonable assumption to proceed.

---

### ✅ Assumption: The Square is the Smallest Square Containing the Point (x, y)

We assume the square is **centered at the origin (0, 0)**, has **sides parallel to the axes**, and is the **smallest square** such that the point (x, y) lies **on or inside** the square.

This means the square has side length:

$$
L = 2 \cdot \max(|x|, |y|)
$$

The square extends from $-L/2$ to $L/2$ in both x and y directions.

---

### 🧮 Step-by-Step Solution

Given a point (x, y), the **maximum distance** from this point to any point on the perimeter of the square is the **Euclidean distance** to the **farthest corner** of the square.

The farthest corner from (x, y) will be the one that is **opposite in direction** to (x, y). That is, if (x, y) is in the first quadrant, the farthest corner is in the third quadrant, and vice versa.

Let’s define the farthest corner as:

$$
(x_{\text{far}}, y_{\text{far}}) = (-L/2, -L/2)
$$

Then the Euclidean distance is:

$$
\text{Distance} = \sqrt{(x + L/2)^2 + (y + L/2)^2}
$$

---

### 🧪 Example: (x, y) = (9, 9)

1. Compute the side length:
   $$
   L = 2 \cdot \max(9, 9) = 18
   $$

2. The farthest corner is at:
   $$
   (-9, -9)
   $$

3. Compute the Euclidean distance:
   $$
   \text{Distance} = \sqrt{(9 + 9)^2 + (9 + 9)^2} = \sqrt{18^2 + 18^2} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 18^2} = 18\sqrt{2} \approx 25.4558
   $$

This is **not** 42, which suggests the example may be using a **different square** or a **different distance metric**.

---

### 🧠 Alternative Interpretation: Square with Side Length 42

If we assume the square has side length 42 (i.e., from -21 to 21 in x and y), and the point (9, 9) is inside it, then the farthest corner is at (-21, -21).

$$
\text{Distance} = \sqrt{(9 + 21)^2 + (9 + 21)^2} = \sqrt{30^2 + 30^2} = \sqrt{2 \cdot 30^2} = 30\sqrt{2} \approx 42.426
$$

This rounds to **42**, which matches the example.

---

### ✅ Final Algorithm

Given a point (x, y), the maximum distance to the perimeter of the square is:

$$
\text{Distance} = \sqrt{(x + L/2)^2 + (y + L/2)^2}
$$

Where:

$$
L = 2 \cdot \max(|x|, |y|)
$$

---

### 🧾 Python Code

```python
import math

def max_distance_to_square_perimeter(x, y):
    L = 2 * max(abs(x), abs(y))
    x_far = -L / 2
    y_far = -L / 2
    distance = math.sqrt((x - x_far)**2 + (y - y_far)**2)
    return round(distance)

# Example usage
x, y = 9, 9
print(max_distance_to_square_perimeter(x, y))  # Output: 25
```

If you want to match the example output of **42**, you must assume a **larger square** (e.g., side length 42), and the point (9, 9) is inside it.
