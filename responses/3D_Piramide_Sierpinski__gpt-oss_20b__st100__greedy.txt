Let  

\[
X_k=\text{the colour of the }k\text{th ball drawn.}
\]

The process is **exchangeable**: the joint distribution of  
\((X_1,X_2,\dots ,X_n)\) is invariant under any permutation of the
indices.  
Consequently, for any fixed \(n\)

\[
P(X_1=\text{white})=P(X_2=\text{white})=\dots =P(X_n=\text{white}).
\]

In particular, for \(n=3\)

\[
P(X_1=\text{white})=P(X_2=\text{white})=P(X_3=\text{white}).
\]

Now consider the event that the first three balls are white:

\[
A=\{X_1=\text{white},\,X_2=\text{white},\,X_3=\text{white}\}.
\]

Because of exchangeability, the probability of \(A\) is the same as the
probability that the first ball is white.  Indeed,

\[
P(A)=P(X_1=\text{white})= \frac13 .
\]

(One can also verify this directly by conditioning on the colour of
the first ball and using the Polya‑urn transition probabilities.)

Hence

\[
\boxed{P(\text{first three balls are white})=\frac13 }.
\]
