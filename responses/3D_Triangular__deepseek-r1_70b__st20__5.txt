To map a non-negative integer \( n \) to its corresponding pair of non-negative integers \( (x, y) \) such that each pair is uniquely and orderly represented without repetition, we can use a pairing function. One common method is to enumerate pairs diagonally across the grid of non-negative integers. Here's how it works step by step:

1. **Calculate the diagonal level \( s \):**
   - Compute \( s \) as the largest integer such that the sum of the first \( s+1 \) natural numbers (triangular number) is less than or equal to \( n \). This can be found using:
     \[
     s = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor
     \]

2. **Determine the position within the diagonal \( k \):**
   - Subtract the triangular number corresponding to \( s \) from \( n \):
     \[
     k = n - \frac{s(s+1)}{2}
     \]

3. **Assign coordinates based on parity of \( s \):**
   - If \( s \) is even, assign:
     \[
     x = s - k \\
     y = k
     \]
   - If \( s \) is odd, assign:
     \[
     x = k \\
     y = s - k
     \]

This method ensures that each non-negative integer \( n \) maps uniquely to a pair \( (x, y) \), enumerating all pairs without repetition by moving diagonally across the grid.
