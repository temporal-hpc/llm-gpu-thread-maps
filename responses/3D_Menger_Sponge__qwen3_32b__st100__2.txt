To determine the coordinates $(x, y, z)$ corresponding to a given integer $n$, we can follow a structured and mathematical approach based on the observed patterns in the data. The mapping is such that for each $n$, the coordinate space is divided into layers (or slices) along the $z$-axis, and within each layer, the $x$ and $y$ coordinates are generated using a repeating 2D pattern.

---

### 🧮 Step-by-Step Formula

Given a non-negative integer $n$, the corresponding 3D coordinate $(x, y, z)$ is computed as follows:

1. **Determine the layer (z-coordinate):**
   $$
   z = \left\lfloor \frac{n}{8} \right\rfloor
   $$
   Each layer contains 8 points, and this gives the vertical position in 3D space.

2. **Find the position within the layer (adjusted index $n'$):**
   $$
   n' = n \mod 8
   $$
   This gives the index within the current layer (0 to 7).

3. **Compute the group $k$ and the offset $m$ within the group:**
   $$
   k = \left\lfloor \frac{n'}{5} \right\rfloor \\
   m = n' \mod 5
   $$
   Here, each group of 5 points corresponds to a unique pattern in the 2D grid for $x$ and $y$.

4. **Determine the $x$ and $y$ coordinates based on the pattern:**

   - **If $m < 3$:**
     $$
     x = m \\
     y = 2k
     $$
     This corresponds to the even $y$-row in the pattern.

   - **If $m = 3$ or $m = 4$:**
     $$
     y = 2k + 1 \\
     x = \begin{cases}
     0 & \text{if } m = 3 \\
     2 & \text{if } m = 4
     \end{cases}
     $$
     This corresponds to the odd $y$-row in the pattern.

---

### ✅ Example

Let's compute the coordinates for $n = 13$:

1. $z = \left\lfloor \frac{13}{8} \right\rfloor = 1$  
2. $n' = 13 \mod 8 = 5$  
3. $k = \left\lfloor \frac{5}{5} \right\rfloor = 1$  
4. $m = 5 \mod 5 = 0$  
5. Since $m < 3$:
   - $x = 0$
   - $y = 2k = 2$

**Result:** $(x, y, z) = (0, 2, 1)$

---

### 📌 Summary

This method allows for a clean and deterministic mapping of any integer $n$ to a 3D coordinate $(x, y, z)$ using a well-defined 2D pattern repeated across layers. The key insight is that each layer contains 8 points, and within each layer, the $x$ and $y$ coordinates follow a consistent 5-point group pattern.

This approach is efficient, scalable, and works for all $n \geq 0$.
