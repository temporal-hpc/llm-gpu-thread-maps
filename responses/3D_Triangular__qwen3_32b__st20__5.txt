To map a given integer $ N $ to coordinates $(x, y, z)$ such that $ x \leq y \leq z $, we utilize the **tetrahedral numbers**. These represent the cumulative count of such triples up to a certain $ z $, and the process to find the corresponding triple involves the following steps:

---

### **Step 1: Determine $ z $**
Find the smallest integer $ z $ such that the **tetrahedral number** $ T(z) $ satisfies:
$$
T(z) = \frac{(z+1)(z+2)(z+3)}{6} \geq N
$$
This gives the "layer" $ z $ in the structure of all valid triples $ (x, y, z) $.

---

### **Step 2: Compute $ N' $**
Once $ z $ is determined, calculate:
$$
N' = N - T(z-1)
$$
This $ N' $ represents the position within the current layer $ z $.

---

### **Step 3: Find $ x $**
Within the layer $ z $, find the largest $ x $ such that the cumulative number of valid pairs $ (x, y) $ with $ x \leq y \leq z $ is less than $ N' $. The cumulative number up to $ x $ is:
$$
\sum_{i=0}^x (z - i + 1) = \frac{(x+1)(2z + 2 - x)}{2}
$$
Solve for the largest $ x $ satisfying:
$$
\sum_{i=0}^x (z - i + 1) < N'
$$

---

### **Step 4: Find $ y $**
After determining $ x $, calculate the remaining count:
$$
N'' = N' - \sum_{i=0}^{x-1} (z - i + 1)
$$
Then, $ y $ is given by:
$$
y = x + (N'' - 1)
$$

---

### **Final Coordinates**
The resulting triple is $ (x, y, z) $, satisfying $ x \leq y \leq z $.

1. Compute $ T(0) = 1 $, $ T(1) = 4 $, $ T(2) = 10 $. Since $ T(1) < 5 \leq T(2) $, $ z = 2 $.
2. $ N' = 5 - T(1) = 1 $.

   $$
   \sum_{i=0}^x (2 - i + 1) < 1
   $$
   For $ x = 0 $, $ \sum = 3 $, so $ x = 0 $.
4. $ N'' = 1 $, so $ y = 0 + (1 - 1) = 0 $.

**Result:** $ (x, y, z) = (0, 0, 2) $.
