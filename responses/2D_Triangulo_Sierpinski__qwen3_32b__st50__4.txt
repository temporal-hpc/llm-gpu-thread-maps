To determine the **x-coordinate** for a given integer $ n $ in a sequence of points $(x, y)$, we follow a structured approach based on the pattern of how these points are generated.

---

### 🧠 **Key Observations**

- For each integer $ y $, there are a certain number of points $(x, y)$.
- For **even $ y $** (e.g., $ y = 0, 2, 4, \dots $), there are **2 points**: $ x = 0 $ and $ x = 1 $.
- For **odd $ y $** (e.g., $ y = 1, 3, 5, \dots $), the number of points **doubles** as $ y $ increases:
  - $ y = 1 \rightarrow 2 $ points
  - $ y = 3 \rightarrow 4 $ points
  - $ y = 5 \rightarrow 4 $ points
  - $ y = 7 \rightarrow 8 $ points
  - $ y = 9 \rightarrow 8 $ points
  - and so on.

- The **x-coordinates** for odd $ y $ follow a **Gray code-like pattern**, where each successive point differs by only one bit in binary representation. The **least significant bit (LSB)** of the Gray code gives the $ x $-coordinate.

---

### 🛠️ **Algorithm**

1. **Find the corresponding $ y $** such that the cumulative number of points up to $ y $ includes $ n $, but not $ y - 1 $.

2. **Compute the position** $ \text{pos} = n - \text{cumulative}(y - 1) - 1 $, where $ \text{cumulative}(y) $ is the total number of points up to $ y $.

3. **Determine the x-coordinate** based on whether $ y $ is even or odd:
   - If $ y $ is **even**, $ x = \text{pos} $
   - If $ y $ is **odd**, compute the **Gray code** for $ \text{pos} $ and take the **LSB**:
     $$
     \text{gray} = \text{pos} \oplus (\text{pos} \gg 1)
     $$
     $$
     x = \text{gray} \mod 2
     $$

---

### ✅ **Example**

Let’s compute the x-coordinate for $ n = 7 $:

- Cumulative counts up to:
  - $ y = 0 \rightarrow 1 $
  - $ y = 1 \rightarrow 3 $
  - $ y = 2 \rightarrow 5 $
  - $ y = 3 \rightarrow 9 $

- Since $ 7 \in [5, 9) $, $ y = 3 $, and $ \text{pos} = 7 - 5 - 1 = 1 $

- $ y = 3 $ is **odd**, so compute Gray code:
  $$
  \text{gray} = 1 \oplus (1 \gg 1) = 1 \oplus 0 = 1
  $$
  $$
  x = 1 \mod 2 = 1
  $$

✅ So, for $ n = 7 $, the x-coordinate is **1**.
