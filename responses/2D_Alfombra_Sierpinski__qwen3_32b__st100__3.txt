To convert a 1D index $ n $ into 2D coordinates $ (x, y) $ in a specific space-filling curve (as observed in the pattern), we can use the following **iterative mathematical formula**. This approach is based on decomposing $ n $ into recursive substructures of size 9, similar to a variant of the Hilbert curve.

---

### **Mathematical Formula**

Given a 1D index $ n $, the corresponding 2D coordinates $ (x, y) $ can be computed as follows:

1. **Determine the recursive level (cycle):**
   $$
   \text{cycle} = \left\lfloor \frac{n}{9} \right\rfloor
   $$

2. **Determine the position within the current level:**
   $$
   p = n \mod 9
   $$

3. **Compute the quadrant within the 3x3 block:**
   $$
   y_{\text{adjust}} = \left\lfloor \frac{p}{3} \right\rfloor, \quad r = p \mod 3
   $$

4. **Compute the adjusted x-coordinate within the 3x3 block:**
   - If $ y_{\text{adjust}} = 0 $:
     $$
     x_{\text{adjust}} = r
     $$
   - If $ y_{\text{adjust}} = 1 $:
     $$
     x_{\text{adjust}} = 2 \cdot (r \mod 2)
     $$
   - If $ y_{\text{adjust}} = 2 $:
     $$
     x_{\text{adjust}} = 1 + r
     $$

5. **Compute the final coordinates:**
   $$
   x = 3 \cdot \text{cycle} + x_{\text{adjust}}, \quad y = 3 \cdot \text{cycle} + y_{\text{adjust}}
   $$

### **Example**

Let’s compute $ (x, y) $ for $ n = 4 $:

- $ \text{cycle} = \left\lfloor \frac{4}{9} \right\rfloor = 0 $
- $ p = 4 \mod 9 = 4 $
- $ y_{\text{adjust}} = \left\lfloor \frac{4}{3} \right\rfloor = 1 $, $ r = 4 \mod 3 = 1 $
- Since $ y_{\text{adjust}} = 1 $, $ x_{\text{adjust}} = 2 \cdot (1 \mod 2) = 2 $
- $ x = 3 \cdot 0 + 2 = 2 $, $ y = 3 \cdot 0 + 1 = 1 $

✅ Result: $ (x, y) = (2, 1) $

---

### **Key Observations**

- The curve is **recursive**, with each level being a 3×3 block of the previous level.
- The coordinate transformation within a 3×3 block depends on the **quadrant** (determined by $ y_{\text{adjust}} $) and the **relative position** (determined by $ r $).
- The formula is **iterative**, allowing for computation without recursion.
