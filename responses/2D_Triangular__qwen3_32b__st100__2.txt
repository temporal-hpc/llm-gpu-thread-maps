To determine the coordinates $(x, y)$ of the $n$-th visited point in the described traversal of an infinite 2D grid, we analyze the structure of the traversal.

---

### 🧠 **Traversal Pattern**

Each "layer" corresponds to a fixed value of $y$. For a given $y$, the points visited are:

$$
(0, y), (1, y), \dots, (y, y)
$$

This means:
- Layer $y$ contains $y+1$ points.
- The total number of points up to and including layer $y$ is the sum of the first $y+1$ natural numbers:

$$
\text{Total points up to layer } y = \frac{(y+1)(y+2)}{2}
$$

Thus, the $n$-th point (starting from 0) falls into the largest layer $y$ such that:

$$
\frac{y(y+1)}{2} \leq n
$$

---

### 🧮 **Mathematical Derivation**

We want to find the largest integer $y$ such that:

$$
\frac{y(y+1)}{2} \leq n
$$

This inequality can be solved using the quadratic formula. Rearranging:

$$
y^2 + y - 2n \leq 0
$$

The positive root of the equation $y^2 + y - 2n = 0$ is:

$$
y = \frac{-1 + \sqrt{1 + 8n}}{2}
$$

Taking the **floor** of this value gives the correct $y$:

$$
y = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor
$$

Once $y$ is determined, the corresponding $x$-coordinate is:

$$
x = n - \frac{y(y+1)}{2}
$$

---

### ✅ **Final Formula**

Given a non-negative integer $n$, the coordinates of the $n$-th point in the traversal are:

$$
\boxed{
\left( n - \frac{y(y+1)}{2}, \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor \right)
}
$$

where $y = \left\lfloor \frac{\sqrt{8n + 1} - 1}{2} \right\rfloor$.
